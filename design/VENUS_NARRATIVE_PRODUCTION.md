# HELIOS: VENUS — narrative production brief

## Campaign logline
A classified Soviet Venus concept is discovered decades later in Russia. An exceptionally handsome, magnetic young engineer whom powerful institutions consider delusional intends to restore the possibility of a living human presence above Venus. He must persuade experts, survive failures, and turn a Russian initiative into an international endeavor.

**Alternative-history notice:** The classified USSR program, its files, and its hypothetical engineering proposals are fictional. Historical Venera missions are real; do not conflate the two.

## Central character — visual and dramatic function
- A strikingly beautiful young man, not a generic older grizzled commander.
- His looks and charisma are narratively relevant: he can command attention, but charm does not solve physics, financing, technical risk or institutional opposition.
- Personal weakness: he mistakes personal conviction for sufficient evidence; he must grow into collaborative leadership.
- Public image: dismissed as a dreamer, a reckless spender, or a brilliant but unreliable person.
- Not yet fixed: name, exact age, relationships, year of birth and links to USSR archival staff. Those require creative approval.

## Cinematic opening (interactive sequence outline)
1. **Cold open, USSR:** close-up on recovered tapes and sealed technical drawings; oppressive fluorescent archive room; fragmentary images of Venus from historic probes. No claim that classified colonization was historical.
2. **Modern Russia:** the young engineer speaks to a hostile review board. The player hears fragments of objections rather than watching a long passive cutscene.
3. **The impossible calculation:** player examines a simplified problem — how to operate an instrument platform at ~50–60 km, deliver supplies, and withstand acidic aerosols.
4. **First commitment:** political and economic choice: small autonomous robotic test, or an early attempt at international coalition. Both require engineering evidence.
5. **Arrival at Venus:** large visual reveal, then immediate operational responsibility; the first aerostat is *fragile*, not a miraculous ready-made city.

## Stage progression and gameplay consequences
| Stage | Narrative event | Mechanical gate |
|---|---|---|
| Archive | Closed USSR program rediscovered | Recover archive |
| Russian scientific hearing | Young protagonist faces sceptics | Research credibility and budget |
| Atmospheric trial | Engineering hypothesis under test | Probe and communication relay |
| International invitation | Russian-only program becomes cooperative | Successful test data before treaty |
| Permanent presence | Repeated shipments and acid damage | Aerostat maintenance and logistics |
| First stable station | Human expansion path becomes credible | Lab survives three stable years |

## Campaign vs session
Campaign has choices, characters, cutscenes and the archival mystery; session uses the same physical/economic rules but replaces story gates with configurable objectives. For now both remain Venus-only.

## Tonal direction
Visually majestic, physically grounded and emotionally ambitious. Avoid magic propulsion, instant planet transformation, implausible geopolitical unanimity and a protagonist who is always right.

## Production stages
1. Lite: text-first branching mission decisions and persistence.
2. Mobile: touch-first atmospheric region exploration and audiovisual feedback.
3. PC: cinematic close-ups, cloud-layer flythrough, institutional interiors, voiced dialogue and complex strategic view.
